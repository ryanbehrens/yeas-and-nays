William Henry Harrison

WhigVice president: John TylerDied 31 days into his term

A general famous for the Battle of Tippecanoe (1811). His 1840 'log cabin and hard cider' campaign sold him as a humble frontiersman, though he came from a wealthy Virginia planter family. He gave the longest inaugural address in history, fell ill three weeks later and died after 31 days, the shortest presidency.

The Treaty of Fort WayneProven1809

As governor of Indiana Territory, Harrison bought about 3 million acres from several tribes for a small payment. Tecumseh and others rejected the deal, leading to the fighting at Tippecanoe.

Outcome: The land passed to the U.S.; the conflict merged into the War of 1812.

Source
Pushing slavery into IndianaProven1800–1809

Harrison, who inherited enslaved people, pressed as territorial governor to allow slavery in Indiana despite the Northwest Ordinance's ban, and backed laws letting settlers hold Black people as long-term 'indentured servants'.

Outcome: Indiana's legislature ended the indenture laws in 1810.

Source
The log-cabin imageProven1840

His campaign's log cabins and cider barrels painted him as a man of the people; he was born on a Virginia plantation and lived in a large house in Ohio.

Outcome: It worked: he won with 53% of the popular vote.

Source

How this page is made. Every president gets the same sections and the same checklist. A controversy is listed if it led to an investigation by Congress, an inspector general, a special or independent counsel or a court, or was a sustained national story covered across the political spectrum. Labels follow the strongest official finding, not media coverage. Debt figures come from the U.S. Treasury; deficits and GDP from the White House budget office and the Commerce Department; unemployment from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (before 1948, yearly estimates by Stanley Lebergott via NBER and the Census Bureau's Historical Statistics); prices from MeasuringWorth; executive orders from the Federal Register and National Archives; clemency from the Justice Department.

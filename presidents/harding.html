29th president of the United States · 1921–1923

Warren G. Harding

RepublicanVice president: Calvin CoolidgeDied in office August 2, 1923

This presidency falls in the 1896–1932 transition, when both parties had progressive and conservative wings; the site uses today's colors from 1897 on, but they are the least certain in these years.

Won the 1920 election in a landslide promising a 'return to normalcy' after World War I. He signed the law creating the federal budget process, cut taxes and spending, hosted the Washington Naval Conference that limited warships, and freed the Socialist Eugene Debs from prison. His administration was one of the most corrupt in U.S. history; most of the scandals, including Teapot Dome, came out after he died of a heart attack in 1923.

Eugene V. DebsCommutationDecember 23, 1921

The crime: A speech urging resistance to the World War I draft (Espionage Act, convicted 1918)

Sentence: Ten years; served about two and a half

Why it drew attention: The Socialist Party leader had won about 900,000 votes for president in 1920 while in prison. Wilson had refused to release him; Harding freed him at Christmas and received him at the White House.

Controversies and scandals

Teapot DomeProven1921–1924

Interior Secretary Albert Fall leased federal oil reserves in Wyoming and California to Harry Sinclair and Edward Doheny without competitive bidding after they gave him about $400,000.

Outcome: Fall was convicted of bribery; the Supreme Court canceled the leases in 1927; Sinclair went to prison for contempt and jury tampering; Doheny was acquitted.

Source
Fraud at the Veterans BureauProven1921–1923

Director Charles Forbes took kickbacks on hospital construction and sold off medical supplies meant for wounded veterans at a fraction of their value.

Outcome: Forbes was convicted in 1925.

Source
The 'Ohio Gang' at the Justice DepartmentCharged1921–1923

Attorney General Harry Daugherty and his associate Jess Smith were accused of selling favors, including liquor permits and protection from prosecution. Smith died by suicide in 1923.

Outcome: Daugherty was tried twice; both juries deadlocked.

Source
Hidden affairsProven1905–1923

Harding had long affairs with Carrie Phillips, whom the Republican Party paid to stay quiet during the 1920 campaign, and with Nan Britton, who said he fathered her daughter.

Outcome: DNA testing confirmed Harding was the father in 2015.

How this page is made. Every president gets the same sections and the same checklist. A controversy is listed if it led to an investigation by Congress, an inspector general, a special or independent counsel or a court, or was a sustained national story covered across the political spectrum. Labels follow the strongest official finding, not media coverage.

Jimmy Carter

DemocraticVice president: Walter MondaleServed one term; lost re-election in 1980

Took office after Watergate promising honest government. He brokered the Camp David Accords between Egypt and Israel, created the Energy and Education departments, and deregulated airlines, trucking and railroads. Double-digit inflation, an energy crisis and the 444-day Iran hostage crisis defined his term, and he lost re-election in a landslide.

Pardons and commutations

A pardon wipes out the punishment for a federal crime; a commutation shortens a sentence but leaves the conviction. Counts are by federal fiscal year. His first-day proclamation pardoning Vietnam-era draft evaders covered hundreds of thousands of people and is not counted individually.

Vietnam-era draft evadersPardon (proclamation)January 21, 1977

The crime: Evading the draft between 1964 and 1973 (military deserters were not included)

Sentence: Up to five years in prison, or exile abroad

Why it drew attention: A campaign promise. Veterans' groups condemned it as rewarding lawbreakers; others said it did not go far enough.

Source
G. Gordon LiddyCommutationApril 12, 1977

The crime: Burglary, conspiracy and illegal wiretapping in the Watergate break-in

Sentence: Up to 20 years; about 4½ served

Why it drew attention: The Watergate break-in's planner. Carter cut his sentence to match the other burglars'.

Source
Patty HearstCommutationFebruary 1, 1979

The crime: Bank robbery (1976), committed after she was kidnapped by a militant group

Sentence: Seven years; 22 months served

Why it drew attention: Supporters argued she was coerced. Clinton later granted her a full pardon.

Source

The crime: The 1954 shooting in the U.S. Capitol that wounded five congressmen, and the 1950 attack on Blair House aimed at President Truman

Sentence: Life and long terms; about 25 years served

Why it drew attention: Their release was welcomed in parts of Puerto Rico and criticized as rewarding political violence.

'Billygate'Disputed1980

The president's brother took money from Libya's government and was accused of lobbying for it.

Outcome: A Senate subcommittee found poor judgment and White House missteps but no illegal acts by the president.

Source
Firing a prosecutor investigating DemocratsAlleged1978

He had a Republican U.S. attorney removed after a Democratic congressman under investigation complained.

Outcome: Carter said he did not know of the investigation; the congressman later pleaded guilty to conflict of interest.

Source
Failed Iran hostage rescueProvenApril 1980

A secret mission to free 52 American hostages in Tehran was aborted in the desert; a helicopter collided with a plane.

Outcome: Eight U.S. servicemen died; a military review found planning and equipment failures.

Source
Soviet grain embargo and Olympic boycottDisputed1980

He halted grain sales to the Soviet Union and led a boycott of the Moscow Olympics after the invasion of Afghanistan.

Outcome: Farmers and athletes bore the costs; whether it pressured the Soviets is debated.

How this page is made. Every president gets the same sections and the same checklist. A controversy is listed if it led to an investigation by Congress, an inspector general, a special or independent counsel or a court, or was a sustained national story covered across the political spectrum. Labels follow the strongest official finding, not media coverage. Debt figures come from the U.S. Treasury; deficits and GDP from the White House budget office and the Commerce Department; unemployment from the Bureau of Labor Statistics; prices from MeasuringWorth; executive orders from the Federal Register and National Archives; clemency from the Justice Department.
